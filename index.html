How the numbers are produced

Source: the National Weather Service API, which needs no key. Each location is resolved from its coordinates to a forecast grid cell roughly 2.5 km across. Two endpoints are used per location: the hourly forecast, which is human-adjusted by the local forecast office, and the raw gridpoint data for sky cover, gusts, humidity, dewpoint and visibility. The walking verdict uses the available readings and forecast wording.

Grid cells are not permanent. NWS states that a coordinate's grid coordinates, and even its assigned office, can change. Cells are therefore resolved from coordinates once a day rather than hardcoded; if one moves, the change is logged to the browser console and the card's subtitle tooltip updates.

These are forecasts, not measurements. There is no weather station at any of these points; the nearest official one sits well inland. Treat every number as what the forecast office expects for a 2.5 km square, not as a reading from that street corner.

This is open source, and it is meant to be taken. Point it at your own coordinates and it works anywhere the National Weather Service forecasts, which is anywhere in the United States. Editing one array at the top of app.js is the whole job: no build, no API key, no account. Take the code on GitHub and make it yours.
